IC 1396 and Mu Cephei — Herschel's Garnet Star
Located in the constellation Cepheus, ++IC 1396++ is a vast emission nebula and active stellar nursery approximately 2,400 light-years from Earth. Its glowing hydrogen clouds are illuminated by massive young stars, while dense regions of gas and dust, including the famous Elephant's Trunk Nebula, continue to give birth to new stars. ++NASA observations++ and [astronomical research](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.3847/1538-3881/ac2cc0/pdf) have revealed young protostars embedded within these clouds.
Appearing near IC 1396 in our sky is the magnificent ++Mu Cephei (μ Cephei)++, famously known as Herschel's Garnet Starfor its striking reddish-orange color. Located approximately 1,307 light-years from Earth, this enormous red supergiant is one of the largest stars visible to the naked eye, with an estimated radius over 1,000 times that of our Sun, although estimates vary. Mu Cephei is also a semiregular variable star whose brightness changes as its vast outer atmosphere pulsates.
Although they appear close together in the night sky, Mu Cephei lies considerably closer to Earth than IC 1396 and is not physically part of the nebula. Together, they offer a fascinating contrast between the birth of new stars within IC 1396 and the late evolutionary stages of Mu Cephei, a massive stellar giant that may eventually end its life as a supernova.
